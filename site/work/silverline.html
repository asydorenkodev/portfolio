Silverline Coffee

Concept website for a coffee shop in Belfast

A winter first screen for a coffee shop on Victoria Square. One frosted glass stands in the ice, and you pick what goes in it by turning a wheel of ingredients.

Type

My role

#01

The task

A coffee shop menu is usually a photo grid. I wanted the drinks to feel like one product with four moods.

The brief was a premium winter screen in silver and ice blue. The cup had to stay the same for every drink, and switching flavours had to feel like a new coffee being poured, not like a slider moving to the next picture.

#02

What I did

I set the art direction, built every image, wrote the copy and coded the page by hand.

The four source photos had four different glasses, one of them a sundae dish on a stem. One glass is drawn from scratch (walls, rim light, condensation, frost) and each drink and its topping are moved into it, so the cup never changes. The ice scene comes from a back-lit photo: the backdrop is the same photo, stretched and blurred like a shallow depth of field, with the sharp ice kept in the middle.

#03

Key features

  • One glass, four drinksThe glass is drawn once and laid over every drink. Only what is inside it changes.
  • A pour, not a slideThe new flavour rises inside the glass behind a soft wave, the topping drops in from above and the name on the cup changes letter by letter.
  • Ingredients on a wheelThe tiles turn round the cup in the direction you switch. One slips under the ice on one side and comes back up on the other.
  • An intro in orderThe ice and the cup come first, then the top bar, then the tiles rise from behind the ice, then the headline.
  • Light from the coffeeA warm glow and a faint reflection under the glass take the colour of the drink you picked.
  • Hovers worth findingTiles tilt towards the pointer and lift the ingredient, the location pin opens into the address and hours, and the headline letters rise near the cursor.

#05

Challenges

  1. Four photos, four glasses

    ProblemThe drinks were shot in a tall tumbler, a pint glass, a milkshake glass and a sundae dish. Side by side they looked like four different shops.

    SolutionThe inside of each glass is traced and mapped into one shape, and the cream, caramel and cherry above the rim are cut out separately. Straws were taken out so every cup reads the same.

  2. Extending a portrait photo

    ProblemThe ice photo is tall and narrow, and a wide screen needs three times its width. Mirroring it made a kaleidoscope of twin mountains.

    SolutionThe backdrop is the same photo blown up and heavily blurred, so the sides read as out of focus. Only the middle stays sharp, at its real size.

  3. A name you can always read

    ProblemWhite lettering on the cup vanished on the cream-heavy drinks.

    SolutionEach drink says whether it is light or dark, and the name switches to a dark chocolate colour on the light ones.

#06

Result

A finished concept hero where choosing a coffee is the fun part of the page.

It’s a concept: the business is made up and the next step would be the menu and the sections below the hero. You can turn the wheel yourself in the live preview ↗.
