Victoria Square Barbers

Concept website for a barbershop in Belfast

A first screen for a barbershop under the glass dome on Victoria Square. The product they finish every cut with stands in the dark, and the visitor’s cursor is the light.

Type
Concept, fictional business
My role
Art direction, image work, copy, build
Scope
Hero screen, desktop and mobile
Live preview
Open the site ↗
#01

The task

Most barbershop sites open on a stock photo of clippers and a price list. Nobody remembers them.

I wanted a first screen that feels like walking into a good shop at night: quiet, dark, one thing in the light. It still had to do the plain jobs: show the prices, the hours, the address and a clear way to book.

#02

What I did

I set the art direction, prepared every image, wrote the copy and built the page by hand.

The bottle, the stones and the smoke were cut out and graded as separate layers, so each one could be lit on its own. The page itself is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework.

#03

Key features

  • The cursor is a torchThe bottle stays a black shape until you bring the light to it. Then the colour, the label and the price tag come up.
  • Water that reflectsThe reflection under the stone is drawn live on a canvas, with slow ripples that follow the light.
  • Next free chairThe booking card works out the next slot from Belfast time and the opening hours, so it never says 14:30 at ten at night.
  • Glass cardsThe barber and booking cards tilt towards the pointer and catch a moving highlight, like real glass.
  • Prices one hover awayThe Services menu opens on a glass panel with every price, so nobody has to scroll to find out what a fade costs.
  • Made for phones tooOn touch screens a slow light sweeps across the bottle once, and a tap turns it on.

#05

Challenges

  1. Dark, but still readable

    ProblemA page built around darkness can easily hide the things people came for: prices, hours, the book button.

    SolutionOnly the picture plays with light. All the text, the cards and the buttons stay fully lit and high-contrast at all times.

  2. People without faces

    ProblemThe barber’s portrait came from a reference photo of someone who never agreed to be in a portfolio.

    SolutionOnly his outline is used: a silhouette with a coloured rim of back light, which also suits the night mood of the page.

  3. Smooth on an ordinary laptop

    ProblemStars, dust, smoke and a live reflection all move at once.

    SolutionEverything shares one animation loop instead of a dozen separate timers, and the motion switches off for visitors who ask for reduced motion.

#06

Result

A finished concept hero that shows how a local shop can look premium without a big budget.

It’s a concept: the business is made up and the next step would be the sections below the hero. You can try the light yourself in the live preview ↗.
