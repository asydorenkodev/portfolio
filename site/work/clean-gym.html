Clean Gym

Concept website for a members’ gym in London

A landing page for a gym in Shoreditch that stops selling memberships at four hundred. Its whole pitch is space and calm, so the page had to feel the same: warm light, slow smoke and very few words.

Type
Concept, fictional business
My role
Art direction, image work, copy, build
Scope
One-page site, five sections
Live preview
Open the site ↗
#01

The task

Premium gyms sell the same thing: no queues for the rack and room to think. Their websites usually look like every budget chain.

The brief to myself: a page that sells calm instead of discounts, explains the membership cap in one sentence, and gets people to book a free visit.

#02

What I did

I set the look, drew the logo and icons, prepared the images, wrote the copy and built the page.

The logo is a weight plate that opens into a C, with the bar running through it. Rocks, plates, the kettlebell and the smoke were cut out and graded so they share one warm light. The page is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

#03

Key features

  • Light where you lookMoving the cursor across a section casts a soft spotlight. Point at a person and their outline lights up fully.
  • Exact hit testThe page checks the real shape of each silhouette, so the light switches on over the body, not over an invisible box around it.
  • Smoke that driftsSmoke is drawn on a canvas from a few painted sprites and rises slowly behind the rocks and the stones.
  • Depth on scrollThe wordmark, the people and the rocks move at different speeds as you scroll, so the hero feels like a room.
  • Glass cardsFeature cards, prices and the booking field tilt towards the pointer and catch a moving highlight.
  • Clear membershipThree plans with the price, the hours and what’s included, and one main action on every card: book a visit.

#05

Challenges

  1. Real people, no faces

    ProblemThe poses came from reference photos of real people, and a portfolio is no place for someone else’s face.

    SolutionOnly the outlines are kept. Each figure is a dark silhouette with a warm rim of back light, which fits the mood better than a photo would.

  2. Hover on a phone

    ProblemThe reveal is built around a cursor, and phones don’t have one.

    SolutionOn touch screens a slow beam of light sweeps across each person while they’re on screen, and a tap lights them fully.

  3. Rich, but calm

    ProblemSmoke, parallax, tilting glass and a custom cursor can turn into noise quickly.

    SolutionEvery effect is slow and low in contrast, and all of them switch off for visitors who ask for reduced motion. The content reads fine without any of it.

#06

Result

A complete one-page concept, from the logo to the booking form.

The gym is made up, so the booking field only checks the email and shows a confirmation. Try the light yourself in the live preview ↗.
